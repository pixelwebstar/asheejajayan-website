# Ideal customer

This is who the website business is for. Later notes will cover what this customer wants, how to reach them, and the words they search. This note is only the person.

## Who he is

A local business owner in India. The business is already open and already has customers. He wants more of them, and he wants them by phone.

The businesses that fit are a restaurant, a salon, a clinic, a tuition center, or a real estate practice. He is educated. He can read a page, follow a price, and ask a straight question.

He treats the website as an investment, not a bill to shrink. He asks what it will return, not what discount he can get. ₹25,000 does not scare him if he believes the money comes back. He walks away from anything that smells like a scam: big percentages with no name attached, pressure, or a price that only appears after he is hooked.

He would rather call than fill in a form. He will fill in a short form if calling is awkward, or if he is with a customer. He does not care how the site is built. He cares whether it brings more calls this month.

## How to sell to him

Sell honestly. If the numbers do not justify a web app, say so. A barber shop does not get a web app when a simple site is what the calls need.

The recommendation is the website and Google Ads together. The site takes the call. The ads bring the people who are already searching.

The first Google Ads campaign is ₹25,000. Based on typical results for businesses that follow the recommended plan, about ₹45,000 spent has come back as about ₹75,000. That is a pattern from those jobs, not a promise of what the next one will return.

## How the site should feel to him

Restraint and elegance. A serious buyer notices that. He does not need a loud page to trust it.

The price does not sit in the opening screen. It sits near the form, or in the ad. The homepage should take three to five minutes to read, then stop.
